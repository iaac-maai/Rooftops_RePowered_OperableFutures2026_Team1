Roofscapes (Re)Powered

Project definition and two-week development plan · 20 November 2026

Research question
How much rooftop solar could a host generate, when might it have surplus, and how would different sharing arrangements use that surplus?

Project definition

Roofscapes (Re)Powered is a planning tool that helps a large-roof owner assess whether rooftop solar could supply their building and nearby participants. The two-week prototype uses CEP9 as the primary end-to-end test case and A160 as a secondary office case. CEP9 provides a documented community and installation; A160 tests an office scenario, including the possibility of little surplus.

The tool is a research and scenario-planning prototype, not a detailed engineering design, operational controller or financial guarantee. Every result must be labeled as observed, published, simulated, synthetic or unknown.

User journey

  1. Select CEP9 or A160 and review the case evidence.
  2. Assess the roof, existing PV and hypothetical rooftop potential.
  3. Model hourly solar generation with documented assumptions.
  4. Explore building-demand scenarios and their uncertainty.
  5. Compare host-only and sharing strategies on compatible inputs.
  6. Review energy outcomes and, when supported, conditional financial outcomes.
  7. Inspect sources and export a scenario summary.

Two-week deliverable

A working CEP9 demonstration with known installation and participant evidence, low/base/high modeled demand, host-only and fixed-share comparisons, energy-balance checks, transparent assumptions and an exportable summary. A160 will show our existing rooftop analysis and missing-data status. Its sharing results should remain pending unless credible aligned scenarios are ready.

Stretch goals: battery sizing, detailed economics and a new A160-trained AI model. They should not delay a reliable core demonstration. Modeled CEP9 demand and sharing outcomes are not measured savings.

AI contribution

Present the existing reference-office surrogate as a demonstration of AI approximating EnergyPlus, with its validation limits visible. Do not claim it predicts CEP9 or A160 demand. A new site-specific model requires suitable simulation data, independent validation and comparison against strong baselines.
